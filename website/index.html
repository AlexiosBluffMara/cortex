What ABM does

Alexios Bluff Mara LLC partners with the ISU College of Applied Science & Technology on applied AI and computational-neuroscience research only (not coursework). The work is hardware-flexible by design: the same code path runs on a single RTX 5090 in our Chicago lab (call sign Ascended Base), on the M4 Max MacBook (Big Apple) sitting next to it, and — through ACCESS-CI partnerships at UIUC and similar institutions — on national HPC nodes when the experiment demands them. Open-source where possible. Local-first by default.

Active named collaborators at ISU CAST: Mangolika Bhattacharya, Sally Xie, and Rosangela Follmann (early-stage, one meeting). Full bios on /about.

"From a single Blackwell card to an HPC partition — same code, different horsepower. The research question dictates the hardware, not the other way around."

What we have, and what we'd build next with funding

The next surface for both Cortex and Mercury is spatial — head-mounted displays where a brain-response visualization actually wraps around the viewer instead of being trapped in a browser tab. Three platforms matter here, and we already own two of them.

Hardware on hand · today
Meta Quest 3
$499 standalone XR headset, 4K+ resolution per eye (2064×2208), Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2, full-color passthrough. We have one in the lab. Meta's Quest for Business developer program issues device-management seats and the Meta Horizon Developer + Meta for Developers tracks both publish quarterly grant calls and SDK access. Rendering Cortex's 20,484-vertex brain mesh in WebXR on Quest 3 is the cheapest credible spatial demo on the public market.
Hardware on hand · today
Apple Vision Pro
$3,499 spatial computer with dual 4K micro-OLED, M2 + R1 chips, true 6DoF eye + hand tracking. We have one. Apple's Apple Developer Program ($99/yr) ships visionOS SDK + RealityKit. Apple Entrepreneur Camp and Apple Investor in People application tracks open periodically; pricing pressure pushes us to ship the AVP demo first because the brand-cred per visit is the highest of the three.
Wishlist · with funding
Google Android XR (Samsung Galaxy XR)
Samsung's Project Moohan / Galaxy XR headset, expected ~$1,800 retail (Samsung's Snapdragon XR2+ Gen 2). Runs Android XR with Gemini and Google's full ecosystem. Highest fidelity at the price- per-pixel point, ideal for an education-sector deployment — undergrad neuro / cognitive-science labs at ISU + UIUC could buy ten of these for the cost of one Vision Pro. Google's Android XR Developer Program is in beta; Google for Startups and Google Cloud for Startups both offer up to $200K in cloud credits to qualifying companies.
Wishlist · with funding · long shot
Google AR Glasses (XR-lite, when it ships)
Google's AR glasses (announced 2026, partner-distributed via Warby Parker / Gentle Monster / Samsung) are the form factor that actually gets a brain-scan demo into a classroom rather than a lab. Pricing TBD, likely $1,200-$2,000. We're in the queue for developer-program access; in the meantime the same WebXR build that ships to Quest will graceful-degrade to glasses-class hardware.

Cost-to-ship math: Quest 3 + AVP combined puts the lab at ~$4,000 of XR hardware on hand. Adding Galaxy XR + Google Glasses would be another ~$3,000-$3,800. Total spatial-computing capex to cover all four major platforms: under $8,000. That's the whole BOM for an education-sector spatial-neuroscience pilot. Compare to the cost of one Hospital MRI hour ($600-$1,200) and the conversation gets short.
