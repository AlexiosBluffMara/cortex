ARTEMIS — RETURNING HUMANS TO THE MOON

NASA's Artemis campaign is the United States' program to land the first
woman and the next man on the Moon and to establish a sustained lunar
presence as a stepping stone toward crewed missions to Mars.

Key elements:

- Space Launch System (SLS): the super-heavy-lift rocket that launches the
  Orion spacecraft. At liftoff it produces roughly 8.8 million pounds of
  thrust, more than the Saturn V that carried the Apollo crews.

- Orion spacecraft: the crew vehicle, designed to carry up to four
  astronauts on missions beyond low Earth orbit, with a reusable crew
  module and a European-built service module.

- Artemis I (uncrewed) tested the SLS and Orion on a flight around the Moon.
  Artemis II carries a crew on a lunar flyby. Artemis III aims to land
  astronauts near the lunar South Pole, a region of scientific interest
  because of water ice in permanently shadowed craters.

- Gateway: a small space station planned in lunar orbit to support surface
  expeditions and serve as a staging point for deep-space exploration.

Why it matters: the lunar South Pole's water ice could be processed into
drinking water, breathable oxygen, and rocket propellant, reducing the
cost of operating far from Earth. The science returned — on lunar geology,
the space environment, and human physiology in deep space — informs the
eventual goal of sending humans to Mars.
